# Sub-task 4: CRO Patterns That Win
## Niche: Personal Coaches - Fitness, Nutrition, Wellness (US/UK)

---

## Most Common Section Sequence (top-converting PT and fitness coach sites)

1. Hero (trainer photo/video + outcome headline + booking CTA)
2. Social proof bar (review count, client results count, certifications)
3. Who this is for (audience specificity — self-selection section)
4. Before/after gallery or client results (the highest-converting visual section)
5. What you get / services overview (online coaching, in-person, nutrition, 12-week etc.)
6. About the trainer (personal story, credentials, philosophy)
7. Testimonials (named, with photo, specific outcome and timeframe)
8. How it works (3-step process: consultation → custom plan → coaching)
9. Pricing or packages (or "apply to work with me" for selective model)
10. FAQ (handles fears: commitment, guarantee, credentials, what's included)
11. Final CTA (book a free consultation or apply)

---

## Hero Composition

- **Imagery subject:** The trainer themselves — ideally in an action shot (training with a client, at the gym, demonstrating form). A professional headshot works but action beats static. Before/after paired images can also anchor the hero if the transformation story is the primary hook.
- **Primary CTA:** "Book a Free Consultation" — the dominant CTA across the top-performing sites. Pairs with a simple booking embed (Calendly, Acuity, or native booking system).
- **Secondary CTA (optional):** "See Client Results" — routes to the testimonials/gallery section for colder visitors who need more proof before booking.
- **Social proof in hero:** Review count ("80 five-star reviews"), client count ("150+ clients coached"), or a single standout result ("Sarah lost 28lbs in 10 weeks").
- **Video:** A 60-90 second intro video in the hero is the highest-converting element for cold traffic in this niche. Personal connection before commitment is essential when the product is access to a specific person.

---

## Trust Stack Ordering

1. Before/after transformation photos (peer proof — "someone like me got results")
2. Named testimonials with specific result + timeframe (Sarah, 28lbs, 10 weeks)
3. Certifications displayed (NASM, ACE, ISSA, REPS — with logo badges)
4. Review count and rating (Google or Facebook widget)
5. Trainer's personal transformation story (authenticity proof — "I've been where you are")
6. Professional photography (signal that this is a real, invested business)
7. Booking system integration (operational proof — a legitimate business has a booking system)

---

## Form Patterns

- **Free consultation booking form:** Embedded calendar (Calendly or Acuity). Name, email, goal (dropdown), preferred time. 3-4 fields. Mobile-optimised.
- **Application form (for selective/"apply to work with me" models):** 5-7 fields: name, email, current situation, goal, timeline, biggest obstacle, how they found the trainer. Increases commitment and filters leads.
- **Form placement:** Directly in the hero (consultation booking widget) and repeated in the final CTA section.
- **Submit button text:** "Book My Free Consultation," "Apply Now," "Claim My Spot."

---

## Sticky Elements

- **Sticky nav bar:** Booking CTA always visible. Many high-performing PT sites keep "Book Free Consult" in a sticky button throughout the scroll.
- **Mobile CTA bar:** Fixed bottom button on mobile — "Book Now" or "Book Free Call" — since mobile is the dominant discovery channel for this niche.
- **Exit intent:** Less common but used for lead magnet capture ("Before you go, get my free 5-day meal plan").

---

## Divergence from Universal Wireframe

The personal trainer niche diverges from the universal wireframe in two significant ways:

1. **Before/after gallery is elevated to a primary section** — not just a trust signal in the testimonials block. For this niche, visual proof of physical transformation is the primary purchase trigger. It belongs above the fold or immediately below the hero.

2. **The trainer's personal story is a core conversion section** — not just an about page afterthought. Buyers are purchasing access to a specific human being. They need to connect with that person's story before they trust them with their body. Sites that bury the trainer story lose to sites that lead with it.
