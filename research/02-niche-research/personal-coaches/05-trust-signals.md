# Sub-task 5: Trust Signals
## Niche: Personal Coaches - Fitness, Nutrition, Wellness (US/UK)

---

## Top 5 Trust Signals (priority order for buyers)

### 1. Before/After Transformation Photos with Named Clients
**Why #1:** No other trust signal in this niche converts like visual proof of results from real clients. Buyers scan for someone who looks like them and achieved the goal they want. The photo must include the client's name (or first name at minimum), their result, and their timeframe. Anonymous transformations are suspected of being staged. Named, specific, dated results are the gold standard.

### 2. Named Video Testimonials with Specific Outcomes
**Why #2:** A 60-second video of a client saying "I lost 18lbs in 8 weeks working with [Trainer]. Here's what was different about this experience" outperforms any written testimonial. Video creates emotional connection and is harder to fake. Even 3-4 genuine video testimonials on a website are a significant competitive advantage.

### 3. Certification Credentials (NASM, ACE, ISSA, REPs Level 3/4)
**Why #3:** Certification is the minimum bar. It filters out unqualified trainers but does not differentiate between qualified ones. Display the certification name and badge, not just the acronym (many buyers don't know what NASM means — "NASM Certified Personal Trainer" is clearer than just the logo). Specialist certifications (pre/postnatal, sports performance, nutrition coaching) differentiate within the credentialled pool.

### 4. Google Reviews Count and Rating
**Why #4:** Buyers check Google before booking. A profile with 80+ reviews at 4.8 stars signals a legitimate business with consistent delivery. The volume matters — 80 four-star reviews beats 10 five-star reviews in buyer perception. A Google reviews widget embedded on the website bridges the check they would do anyway.

### 5. Professional Photography
**Why #5:** Stock photos are recognised instantly and signal inauthenticity. A website with professional photos of the actual trainer, their training environment, and ideally their clients signals investment and legitimacy. In a market where many trainers have DIY Instagram-style websites, professional photography is a visible differentiator.

## Niche-Specific Certifications and Badges

| Certification | Market | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| NASM (National Academy of Sports Medicine) | US | Most recognised PT cert in US market |
| ACE (American Council on Exercise) | US | Second most recognised US cert |
| ISSA (International Sports Sciences Association) | US/UK | Widely accepted online coaching cert |
| REPs Level 3 / Level 4 | UK | UK Register of Exercise Professionals — required for most UK gym work |
| CIMSPA | UK | Chartered Institute for the Management of Sport and Physical Activity |
| Precision Nutrition (PN1/PN2) | US/UK | Nutrition coaching certification — strong credibility in wellness space |
| Pre/Postnatal Specialist | US/UK | Specialist cert that unlocks a highly specific and loyal client segment |

## Press / "As Seen In" Expectations

Press is less relevant for local personal trainers. For online coaches, media mentions (Men's Health, Women's Health, Runner's World, Self, Glamour, or major fitness influencer features) carry weight. If the client has any, it must be displayed prominently. More commonly: social media following counts serve as a proxy for authority in this niche.

---

## Photo / Video Testimonial Expectations

- **Before/after photos:** The most important visual asset. Must be real clients with name + result + timeframe. Side-by-side format standard.
- **Video testimonials:** 60-90 second client testimonials. 3+ on the website sets the site apart from competitors.
- **Written testimonials:** Named + photo + specific result + optional role or location. Generic ratings are ignored.
- **Progress documentation (mid-journey):** Some high-performing sites show 4-week, 8-week, 12-week progress photos from the same client — demonstrating the process, not just the end result. Builds more trust than before/after alone.
