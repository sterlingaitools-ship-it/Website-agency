# Sub-task 4: CRO Patterns That Win
## Niche: Sales Trainers and Event Hosts (US/UK)

---

## Most Common Section Sequence (sales trainer / event host sites)

1. Hero (outcome headline + trainer photo/video + primary CTA)
2. Social proof bar (number of students, revenue generated by graduates, logos of companies trained)
3. The problem (agitation section — what life looks like without this training)
4. The solution (the program/event and what makes it different)
5. Curriculum / what you'll learn (specific modules, not vague transformation claims)
6. Who it's for (ideal student description — makes unqualified leads self-select out)
7. Trainer authority section (track record, named results, personal story)
8. Testimonials (named, with photo, specific result, industry/role)
9. Pricing / offer stack
10. FAQ (handles objections — "Is this live?", "What if I can't make it?", "Is there a guarantee?")
11. Final CTA + urgency element (countdown timer, seats remaining, enrolment deadline)

---

## Hero Composition

- **Imagery subject:** The trainer in a high-energy speaking context (on stage, in front of a whiteboard, or looking directly at camera). Event photos with packed rooms are the most powerful. Stock images kill credibility in this niche.
- **Primary CTA:** "Reserve My Seat," "Book a Free Call," or "Apply Now." One CTA only.
- **Secondary CTA (optional):** "Watch the free training first" for cold traffic who need warming.
- **Social proof in hero:** Number of students trained, or a one-line result quote from a graduate.
- **Video:** A 60-90 second trainer intro video in the hero dramatically improves conversion for cold traffic. Buyers are purchasing access to a person — they need to see and hear that person before committing.

---

## Trust Stack Ordering

1. Number of students / graduates trained (volume proof)
2. Named testimonials with specific revenue/result outcomes
3. Trainer's personal sales track record (the "I've done this myself" proof)
4. Company or brand logos (companies whose teams have been trained)
5. Press / media mentions (secondary — less important than peer proof in this niche)
6. Money-back guarantee badge
7. Certification or association memberships (least important here; buyers don't care about certifications the way roofing customers care about GAF)

---

## Form Patterns

- **Short form for free events:** Name + Email only. Lowest friction.
- **Application form for high-ticket programmes:** 5-8 fields including role, current income, goal income, main challenge. Filters leads + increases commitment.
- **Form placement:** Hero for low-ticket/free events. Bottom of long-form sales page for high-ticket after the full pitch has been delivered.
- **Submit button text:** "Reserve My Free Seat," "Apply for the Programme," "Get Instant Access."

---

## Sticky Elements

- **Sticky CTA bar:** Countdown timer + CTA button at the top or bottom as user scrolls. Standard on event registration pages.
- **Exit intent popup:** More common in this niche than in trades. Typically offers a free lead magnet or a reduced-commitment entry ("Watch the free training instead").
- **Sticky nav:** Usually minimal — long-form sales pages often remove the nav entirely to prevent distraction.

---

## Divergence from Universal Wireframe

The biggest divergence: sales trainer and event host sites use a long-form sales page structure (1,500-5,000 words on a single scrolling page) rather than a short homepage. This is because the buyer needs significant conviction-building before committing $500-$5,000. The universal wireframe assumes a shorter decision cycle. Here, the friction is much higher and the page must handle objections inline.

The second divergence: urgency mechanics (countdown timers, seats remaining) are standard practice in this niche and expected by buyers. In most niches these feel manipulative. Here they are a normal part of the purchase experience and not using them signals inexperience.
