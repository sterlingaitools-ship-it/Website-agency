# Copywriting Contract: Trades - Roofing, Plumbing, Electrical

## 1. Voice Grammar

Voice in one sentence: direct and plain, speaking to a homeowner about the decision to hire a contractor they can trust with one of the most expensive repairs their home will ever need.

Voice principles:

**1. Every sentence earns trust before it asks for anything.**

Avoid: "We are the premier roofing solution provider in the region."
Use: "We have replaced 400+ roofs in this area since 2008. Our license number is on every page."

**2. Short sentences. One idea per sentence. No compound claims.**

Avoid: "Our expert team of certified professionals delivers high-quality, cost-effective roofing solutions tailored to your unique needs."
Use: "We are GAF Master Elite certified. That puts us in the top 3% of roofers in the US. We offer a 25-year workmanship warranty."

**3. Specificity over generality. Numbers beat adjectives.**

Avoid: "Hundreds of satisfied customers trust us."
Use: "247 five-star reviews. Average rating: 4.9. Names and photos on the Reviews page."

**4. Name the fear before selling the solution.**

Avoid: "We provide comprehensive roofing services."
Use: "You have heard the horror stories. Contractors who take deposits and disappear. We are licensed, insured, and our BBB A+ rating is public record. Check it before you call."

**5. The homeowner is not naive. Do not talk down to them.**

Avoid: "Roofing can be a complicated process."
Use: "Your roof has three layers. Here is what each one does, and which one we found damaged."

**6. No fluff between the claim and the proof.**

Avoid: "We are passionate about delivering outstanding results for every homeowner we work with."
Use: "We show up on the day we say we will. That is the number one complaint about contractors in this area. We take it seriously."

## 2. Banned Phrases (Niche-Specific Additions)

Beyond the universal blocklist, the following phrases are banned in this niche. See also `copy-blocklist-additions.md`.

Phrases that pattern-match contractor scam language or signal AI copy to homeowners who have been burned before:

- "best price guaranteed" (suggests race to bottom, undermines quality positioning)
- "limited time offer" (used by fly-by-night storm chasers)
- "act now" (pressure tactic associated with dishonest contractors)
- "industry-leading" (vague, unprovable, sounds like every other roofer)
- "we do it all" (undermines roofing specialisation)
- "quality craftsmanship" (every contractor says this; it signals nothing)
- "your satisfaction is our priority" (AI-generated padding, means nothing)
- "trusted by homeowners" (too generic, replace with a count)
- "exceptional service" (replace with a specific claim)
- "competitive pricing" (the homeowner has no reference point; be specific)
- "years of experience" (replace with the actual number and a specific outcome)

## 3. Preferred Phrases (Niche-Specific Authenticity Signals)

These phrases appear in top-performing roofing sites written by real practitioners. Use them.

- "free inspection, no obligation" (not "free estimate" alone - inspection is the entry point)
- "we handle the insurance claim from start to finish"
- "GAF Master Elite - top 3% of US roofers"
- "licensed, insured, and on the books" (UK variant: "fully insured, VAT registered")
- "we do not start until you sign off on the quote"
- "same-day emergency tarping"
- "25-year workmanship warranty in writing"
- "we clean up completely before we leave"
- "real photos of real roofs we have done in your area"
- "our license number is on this page - look it up"
- "we answer the phone seven days a week"
- "storm damage? call us before you call the insurer"

## 4. Tone Calibration by Sub-Segment

**Planned replacement (not urgent):** The homeowner is in research mode, getting three quotes, comparing contractors. They have time. Tone is informative and confident. Lead with credentials and completed project volume. Price-range guidance helps.

**Emergency repair (active leak or storm damage):** The homeowner is stressed and time-pressed. Tone is calm and action-oriented. Lead with availability (24/7, same-day), then proof. Do not make them read a paragraph before they see the phone number.

**Insurance claim situation:** The homeowner is confused and potentially afraid of being caught in a dispute between their insurer and the contractor. Tone is reassuring and process-focused. Explain each step. Use the phrase "we handle this for you" often.

**Spring/fall seasonal inspection:** The homeowner is in planning mode. Tone is preventive and educational. The free inspection CTA works well here. No urgency required.

## 5. Section-by-Section Copy Frameworks

### Hero Section

**H1 formula:** `{reviewCount} Five-Star Reviews. {city}'s Most Trusted Roofing Contractor.`
Proof-first. No tagline. No claim without a number attached.

**Subhead formula:** `Licensed, insured, and GAF certified. Serving {city} and surrounding areas since {foundedYear}.`
Credentials + geography + tenure. Three trust signals in one sentence.

**Form heading:** `Get Your Free Estimate`
Locked. Do not change. Niche research confirms this is the highest-converting form header.

**Social proof eyebrow (above H1):** `★★★★★ {reviewCount} Five-Star Reviews`
Renders before the headline. This is the first thing the homeowner reads.

**Guardrails for Hero:**
- No taglines. No brand slogans. Only proof and credentials.
- The headline must include a review count or certification signal.
- Do not use "welcome to" constructions.
- Do not start the headline with the company name.

---

### Trust Bar

**Item labels:**
1. `{reviewCount} Google Reviews` - number always precedes the label
2. `GAF Certified` - add: "Top 3% of US Roofers"
3. `BBB Accredited` - link to BBB profile
4. `Licensed and Insured` - show the license number below the label
5. `{yearsInBusiness} Years Serving {city}`

**Guardrails for Trust Bar:**
- Never show a badge without a label. Homeowners do not know what GAF means without context.
- License number must be visible, not just "Licensed."
- Year count must be a real number, not "over a decade."

---

### Services Grid

**Section H2 formula:** `Our Roofing Services`

**Card body formula (one line per card):**
`{service name} - {one line describing the pain it solves or the benefit it delivers}`

Example: "Storm Damage and Insurance Claims - We document the damage, meet the adjuster, and handle the entire claim."

**Guardrails for Services:**
- Each card headline is the service name. No creative rewrites.
- One line of supporting copy per card. No more.
- "Learn More" link on every card. Homeowners who want detail need a path.

---

### Why Us Section

**Section H2 formula:** `Why {city} Homeowners Choose Us`

**Differentiator copy formula per item:**
- Heading: the differentiator in 3-5 words
- Body: one sentence of proof supporting the claim

Example:
- Heading: "GAF Master Elite Certified"
- Body: "Less than 3% of US roofing contractors qualify. We are one of them."

**Guardrails for Why Us:**
- Every differentiator requires a supporting fact or proof.
- Do not use generic differentiators that every contractor could claim ("customer service", "experienced team").
- Four items only. The design is a four-column strip.

---

### Process Section

**Section H2 formula:** `How It Works`

**Step copy formula:**
- Step heading: action noun phrase (Free Inspection / Custom Quote / Expert Installation)
- Step body: two sentences max. First sentence: what we do. Second sentence: what the homeowner gets.

Example step 1:
- Heading: "Free Inspection"
- Body: "We visit your property at no cost. You get a written report with photos before we discuss anything else."

**Guardrails for Process:**
- Three steps only. Research confirms 3-step is the niche standard.
- No-obligation badge beneath the steps is required.
- Do not add a fourth step unless a client's specific process genuinely has four stages.

---

### Reviews Section

**Section H2 formula:** `What {city} Homeowners Say About Us`

**Review card formula:**
- Quote: specific outcome, not generic praise. Must include at least one of: a service type, a timeline, an insurance angle, or a certification reference.
- Name: first name + last initial (e.g. Sarah T.)
- Location: city and state
- Outcome tag: pill label from the niche's tag set (Roof Replacement / Storm Damage / Insurance Claim / Roof Repair)

**Real reviews exist:** Display verbatim. Do not edit. Truncate at 180 characters with expand link.

**Fewer than 4 real reviews:** Do not fabricate reviews. Contact the client to gather testimonials. The proposal should list review gathering as a deliverable.

**Guardrails for Reviews:**
- No anonymous reviews. Name required.
- No star ratings that contradict the review text.
- No outcome tags invented without the homeowner's confirmation.

---

### Gallery Section

**Section H2 formula:** `Our Recent Work`

**Caption formula:** `{serviceType} - {citySuburb}` (e.g. "Roof Replacement - Marietta, GA")

**Guardrails for Gallery:**
- Every photo needs a location tag.
- Before-and-after pairs are mandatory for the homepage (3 pairs).
- No stock photos. Ever.

---

### Service Areas Section

**Section H2 formula:** `Proudly Serving {region}`

**Pill label format:** `{City Name}, {State Abbreviation}` (e.g. "Atlanta, GA")

**Guardrails for Service Areas:**
- Pill links must go to live city pages. Broken links damage SEO.
- List only cities where the contractor actually works.

---

### Final CTA Band

**H2 formula:** `Ready to Get Started? Get Your Free Roof Estimate Today.`

**Subhead formula:** `No obligation. No pressure. Just a clear, honest quote.`

**Button label:** `Get Your Free Estimate` (locked, no variation)

**Secondary line:** `Or call us:` + phone number as click-to-call

**Guardrails for Final CTA:**
- This section is the catch-net for homeowners who scrolled past the hero form.
- Subhead must contain the words "no obligation."
- Do not add new claims here. Repeat the offer, nothing more.

---

### Footer

**Tagline formula (optional):** `Licensed. Insured. Certified. On time.`

**License line:** `Lic. #` + license number (exact format)

**Guardrails for Footer:**
- License number must be visible in the footer.
- Email and phone must both be present.
- Copyright year must be the current year.

## 7. Review Guardrails

**When real reviews exist (50+ Google reviews at 4.5+):**
- Display verbatim. No editing.
- Truncate quote to 180 characters max. Add "Read more" link.
- Always attribute: first name, last initial, city, state.
- Show source icon (Google, BBB, etc.).
- Date is optional but adds credibility.

**When real reviews are thin (fewer than 10):**
- Do not fabricate reviews.
- Flag to the student in the proposal: review gathering is a prerequisite.
- Placeholder card: "We are building our online presence. Call us and we will connect you with a recent customer who can speak to their experience."
- Gather testimonials directly from the client's existing customers before launch.

**What generated reviews must never claim:**
- A specific certification the client does not hold
- A price or price range
- A guarantee period the client has not confirmed
- A named competitor comparison

## 8. Location-Page Copy Framework

**H1 pattern:** `Roofing Contractor in {City}, {State}`

**Intro paragraph rules (150-200 words):**
1. Open with a reference to the homeowner's specific situation in that city (climate, recent weather events, neighbourhood growth).
2. Name the service area explicitly.
3. Reference one local landmark or neighbourhood name.
4. Include a certification claim with proof (GAF Master Elite, BBB A+).
5. Close with the free inspection CTA.

**Internal linking pattern:**
- Link to each service page from the city page.
- Link from city page to 3-4 neighbouring city pages.
- Link back to homepage.

**Word count minimum:** 400 words. Google's local page threshold.

---

## 9. Service Page Copy Framework

**H1 pattern:** `{Service Name} in {City}, {State}`

**Hero paragraph (60-80 words):**
Open with the homeowner's pain point. Name the specific problem (leak, storm, age, insurance). Immediately transition to the solution. Include the free inspection CTA.

**Required H2 sections:**
1. What {Service Name} Covers
2. What to Expect (timeline, day-of process)
3. Materials We Use (for replacement pages: shingle brands, GAF, Owens Corning)
4. Warranty
5. Frequently Asked Questions

**Word count minimum:** 600 words per service page.

**Schema requirement:** Service + LocalBusiness + AggregateRating + FAQPage on every service page.

## 12. Em-Dash and Smart-Quote Audit

Zero em-dashes. This is a hard fail. Use a period or a comma instead.

Smart quotes throughout. Straight quotes (" ") are a typography fail. Use curly quotes (" ").

`python3 tools/copy-lint.py --check` must pass before delivery. Run it after every copy pass.

## 13. Quality Bar (Niche-Specific Gates)

1. Every review card on the homepage includes a specific outcome (not just "great service").
2. Every city page references at least one local detail specific to that city.
3. The hero headline contains a review count or certification reference. No exceptions.
4. The license number is visible in at least two places on every page (nav bar or footer, plus trust bar).
5. Every service page has an FAQ section with FAQPage schema.
